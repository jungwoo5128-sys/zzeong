## Background

As you study Japanese, your vocabulary and grammar grow, but there is still no one to actually talk to in Japanese. Textbook dialogues get boring fast, and there is nobody around to correct your mistakes.

The name Shabero comes from しゃべる (to chat). The goal was an app you reopen because you want to talk to the characters, not one you open to study. I narrowed what I wanted to test down to two questions: do people want to keep talking to the characters, and do the corrections actually help them learn?

## What's inside

- Four characters with different personalities and ways of speaking: Mio, a barista from Osaka; Ren, a book editor; Shizuku, who runs a used bookstore in Kyoto; and Hinata, a university student working part-time at an izakaya.
- No Japanese keyboard needed. Write in English, Korean, or romaji and it is understood as Japanese.
- Corrections: your Japanese is rewritten naturally, with the reason explained.
- Explanations: tap a message to open its translation, furigana, pronunciation, and word meanings.
- A word list, a mistake notebook with review, and role-play scenes like ordering at a café or asking for directions.
- Difficulty matched to your level, from complete beginner to JLPT N1. The interface is available in Korean and English.

## Design decisions

- Learning features stay outside the chat bubbles: character replies never contain corrections or translations. They open quietly only when the user taps, so the conversation stays immersive.
- Pronunciation comes from code, not the model: for beginners, the readings and furigana are generated with kuromoji morphological analysis and rules, because a model reads the same sentence differently each time.
- Characters remember, and they know the time: older conversations are summarized into a memory note for each character, so they pick up like a friend would. The user's local time is matched against each character's schedule to prevent slips like saying "I'm at the shop now" on a day off.
- Costs are capped on the server: the Claude API is only called server-side, and per-user and global daily message limits are counted in Firestore transactions.
- The web app becomes the mobile app: the same Next.js web app is wrapped with Capacitor and released as an iPhone app (App Store, 2026.11).
